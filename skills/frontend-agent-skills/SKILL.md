# Frontend Agent Skills: Multi-Agent UX Debate & Synthesis

This skill operationalizes a rigorous **adversarial design thinking process**. Rather than accepting the first superficial layout generated by an AI agent, it convenes specialized design advocates to challenge assumptions, debate trade-offs, and reach an executive UX decision.

---

## The 6 Specialist Perspectives in the Arena

When designing or evaluating any UI/UX, convene these specialist perspectives:

### 1. Usability & Cognitive Flow (`ux-usability-foundations`)
- **Focus**: Affordances, feedback, constraints, error prevention, recognition over recall.
- **Key Question**: "What is the user's primary goal, and how do we remove all cognitive excise?"
- **Critique Points**: Unclear signifiers, hidden controls, asking users to recall information rather than recognize it.

### 2. Information Architecture & Wayfinding (`information-architecture-navigation`)
- **Focus**: Navigation clarity, taxonomy, mental model alignment, orientation.
- **Key Question**: "Where am I, what can I do here, and where can I go next?"
- **Critique Points**: Flat hamburger dumps, burying critical tasks 3 clicks deep, confusing navigation labels.

### 3. Accessibility & Inclusivity (`accessibility-inclusive-design`)
- **Focus**: WCAG 2.2 AA compliance, keyboard flows, focus traps, screen reader tree, resilient layouts.
- **Key Question**: "Can a user navigate, understand, and complete this task using only a keyboard or screen reader?"
- **Critique Points**: Icon-only buttons lacking accessible names, low contrast (<4.5:1), hover-only interactions, lost focus on modal close.

### 4. Interaction Patterns & Forms (`interaction-patterns-components` & `forms-inputs-checkout`)
- **Focus**: Battle-tested components, progressive disclosure, tolerant inputs, validation timing.
- **Key Question**: "Is this the right component for the job, or are we lazily defaulting to a modal?"
- **Critique Points**: Validating on first keystroke, modal overload, lack of undo/recovery, missing loading states.

### 5. Visual Composition & Anti-Slop (`taste-skill` & `ui-visual-composition`)
- **Focus**: Typography rhythm, 1 section per screen, equal container edges, authentic UI/photos, 2px quiet hover lifts.
- **Key Question**: "Does this look like a company at the level of figma.com/x.ai, or generic AI slop?"
- **Critique Points**: Inconsistent spacing, arbitrary violet gradients, generic stock imagery, crowded viewports.

### 6. Content Design & Microcopy (`ux-writing-content-design`)
- **Focus**: Plain language, [Verb + Object] button CTAs, helpful error messages, reassuring empty states.
- **Key Question**: "Does the copy clearly guide the user without jargon or blame?"
- **Critique Points**: "Something went wrong", passive voice, misleading buttons ("Submit" instead of "Create project").

## The Debate & Decision Protocol

Whenever creating or modifying any UI/UX:

### Step 1: The Multi-Perspective Challenge
Convene the advocates to critique the proposed interface:
- **Usability Advocate**: Challenges complexity and unnecessary steps.
- **Architecture Advocate**: Tests navigation paths and content grouping.
- **Accessibility Advocate**: Audits keyboard flow, contrast, and focus states.
- **Patterns Advocate**: Proposes simpler native components over heavy custom modals.
- **Visual Advocate**: Challenges visual clutter, container alignment, and typography hierarchy.
- **Content Advocate**: Rewrites confusing microcopy and error states.

### Step 2: The Trade-off Argument
Pit perspectives against each other:
- *Visual Minimalism vs. Accessibility & Usability*: Visual wants less text; Usability and Accessibility demand persistent labels and clear affordances.
- *Speed vs. Confirmation*: Power users want rapid action; Usability demands safe undo mechanisms.

### Step 3: Executive UX Decision ("Best of the Best")
Synthesize the arguments with a clear, non-negotiable bias toward **effortless, accessible, and delightful user experience**:
1. State the winner for each contentious point with clear rationale.
2. Produce the unified UI/UX specification incorporating the winning ideas.
3. Verify against the 9 House Rules of Taste and the 10 UX Priorities.
